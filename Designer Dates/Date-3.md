### Designer Date: Software Stacks, Spreadsheets, and Scaling Communities

![alt text](./images/DATE3.jpeg)


#### The Outing

For this week's date, I met online with a second degree connection to discuss the use of software at a non-profit organization. To hit this week’s requirement of exploring my domain (platform architecture and technical product management), through a mutual connection, I set up an online meeting with the Head of Community at an undisclosed non-profit. I wanted to step back from the immediate demands of my specific projects and just casually talk to an expert about how they actually interact with the systems they use to manage their network every day.

#### The Questions & The Interaction

Rather than a formal stakeholder interview, I framed it as a casual exploration of their day-to-day relationship with technology and how they navigate their digital environment as a community leader.

#### The Questions

Since the prompt was to ask three questions about my domain, I focused on the intersection of systems, emerging tech, and human experience:

1. "How does the use of AI currently influence your day-to-day workflow?"
2. "If you had to map out your digital life right now, what software tools are you actually relying on?"
3. "How has this specific software stack—or the lack of a unified one—affected the community experience?"

#### The Interaction

The answers were incredibly revealing about the reality of legacy systems in the non-profit space. When I asked about AI, they noted that it’s purely ad hoc—people are using it here and there on their own for quick tasks, but there's no systemic integration.

When we got to their software stack, it sounded like a highly fragmented puzzle. They mentioned bouncing between tools like Blackbaud, Slate, Mailchimp, and YAMM, but the true glue holding everything together was just a massive amount of spreadsheets.

The most insightful part of the conversation was their answer to my third question. They admitted that the organization feels technologically behind. Because the community is currently on the smaller side, they survive by doing a lot of things manually. However, they are acutely aware that this manual labor is a ticking clock. As the community grows and they look to attract new talent, this fragmented, highly manual process is going to become a major bottleneck.

#### Reflection

This outing reinforced a core concept in system design for me: manual effort masks software deficiencies, but only up to a point. Small communities can run on spreadsheets and sheer human willpower, but true growth requires structural support. It was a great reminder that when designing a centralized platform, the goal is to alleviate the invisible manual burden that is keeping a community from scaling naturally. Taking the time to sit and just talk about these systems conceptually was a highly valuable mental reset.
